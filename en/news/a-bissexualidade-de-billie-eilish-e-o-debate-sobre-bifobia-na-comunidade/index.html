Billie Eilish's bisexuality and the debate on biphobia within the community

Recent discussions about Billie Eilish's love life expose recurring prejudices against bisexual people within the LGBTQIA+ community itself.

By Billie Global BR Newsroom · Jun 11, 2025 · 2 min read

Billie Eilish's love life has once again become the target of heated debates on social media after the singer was spotted with actor Nat Wolff. The negative backlash from some internet users highlighted an old and painful problem: the constant invalidation of bisexuality. For many fans, seeing the artist in a relationship with a man was mistakenly interpreted as a betrayal of the album Hit Me Hard and Soft and her own public identity, which she revealed herself in 2023.

This type of judgment reflects the internalized biphobia that permeates society and, surprisingly, also the LGBTQIA+ movement. Over the years, various artists who experience different affective attractions face the stigma of constantly having to prove who they are, as if being in a relationship with someone of the opposite sex canceled out attraction to other genders. The American singer, who has built an authentic musical career filled with plural references, owes no explanations about who she chooses to get involved with.

The concept of fluidity and attraction to more than one gender are historical pillars of bisexual visibility, reinforced by decades of activism and foundational texts. However, the weight of mononormativity means bi people are frequently viewed with suspicion, labeled as indecisive, or accused of taking advantage of minority agendas. This pressure for constant validation deeply affects those at the base of the community who are trying to cope with the fear of the closet.

The debate surrounding Billie Eilish raises important questions about privilege, visibility, and the way public figures are held accountable for their relationships. Although the global star has a solid structure to deal with the wave of toxic comments, the same hostility on the internet directly targets anonymous youths seeking acceptance. The demand for queer people to live under a rigid standard of appearance or affective choice is harmful and disregards the complexity of plural experiences.

It is essential to recognize that someone's sexual orientation is not reduced to the gender of the person they share their life with at a given moment. The singer remains part of the community, just like millions of people who face alarming statistics of violence and invisibility daily. The fight for rights and inclusion requires unity, not the questioning of others' legitimacy based on outdated prejudices.

Ultimately, no bisexual person needs to account for their intimacy to be accepted or considered a legitimate part of the movement. Stigmas that reduce identities to phases or conveniences must be fought at their root, including within spaces that should offer safety and respect. Billie Eilish did not trick anyone, and the community needs to learn to embrace all of its letters without demanding proofs of affection.

Billie Eilish explores maturity and self-discovery in Hit Me Hard and Soft

The singer's third album marks a phase of artistic emancipation, addressing sexuality and emotional complexities without constraints.

By Billie Global BR Newsroom · May 20, 2024 · 2 min read

Released as one of the most striking works of her career, Billie Eilish's third album, Hit Me Hard and Soft, symbolizes an important transition in the North American artist's career. Ever since she burst onto the global music scene as a teenager, accumulating records, nine Grammy Awards, and two Oscar statuettes, the singer has had to deal with the overwhelming weight of fame under international spotlights.

With production by her brother and creative partner Finneas, the record functions as a document of emancipation and maturity. Unlike her previous projects, which oscillated between the gloomy and the attempt to mask aesthetic and social pressures, this new chapter features an artist more comfortable with her own identity, openly exploring themes such as self-discovery and affective fluidity.

The opening of the tracklist makes the intentions of this new phase clear. In tracks like Skinny and Lunch, Billie directly and unashamedly addresses her own sexuality and the shattering of others' expectations regarding her body. The sound flows fluidly between delicate acoustic ballads, guitar arrangements, and bold electronic beats, demonstrating the artistic versatility that the siblings have been refining over the years.

The project also dives into deep vulnerabilities, narrating heartbreaks and emotional pains with a confessional tone. Songs like Birds of a Feather bring a luminous pop atmosphere with retro influences, while dramatic moments like The Greatest elevate the emotional charge of the record to its peak, displaying striking and visceral vocal power.

In the second half, the album takes experimental paths and darker textures, subtly recalling the aesthetic roots of the singer's acclaimed debut album. This alternation between expansive moments and minimalist passages creates its own dynamic, even though some critics point out a certain horizontality when listening to the tracks consecutively.

The project's cover, which depicts the artist submerged, perfectly sums up the work's thematic proposal: a deep dive into unknown waters that results in lightness and renewal. Without rigid formulas, Billie Eilish delivers a bittersweet record about growing up, making mistakes, and loving, reaffirming her position of absolute prominence in contemporary pop music.

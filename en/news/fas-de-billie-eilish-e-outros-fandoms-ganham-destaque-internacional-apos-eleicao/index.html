Billie Eilish fans and other fandoms gain international prominence after election

The international press pointed to the mobilization of fan communities, including admirers of Billie Eilish, as a major factor in Lula's victory in the 2026 Elections.

By Billie Global BR Newsroom · Oct 26, 2026 · 2 min read

The victory of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva over Flávio Bolsonaro in the runoff of the 2026 Elections, held this Sunday, continues to generate repercussions inside and outside the country. Major international outlets, such as the American magazine Variety and the British newspaper The Guardian, highlighted an unprecedented phenomenon in national politics: the strong and organized participation of major pop music stars' fandoms, including communities dedicated to Billie Eilish, Taylor Swift, and Lady Gaga, in the final stretch of the presidential race.

The involvement of these networks began to gain traction right after the first round, which took place on October 4th, when the Workers' Party campaign sought new alternatives to broaden dialogue with young voters and combat disadvantages on digital platforms. More than 600 Brazilian fan groups united to declare support for the progressive project, transforming their entertainment pages and musical update channels into active hubs of militancy and the dissemination of fact-checked information about the election.

The coordination centered around the Fandoms pelo Brasil collective, which brings together about 250 different fan clubs. Overcoming traditional internet rivalries, these communities organized expressive public events, such as the March for Democracy held in capitals like São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro. Beyond the streets, fans brought crucial agendas to live streams with government leaders, discussing social rights and the future of culture with good humor and references to the universes of their favorite artists.

The prior relationship between these communities and the current federal administration also helped strengthen political ties. Months before the election, in August, the government sanctioned new rules for ticket sales at concerts in the country, a longstanding demand of fan collectives that suffered from abusive prices and the action of scalpers. For fan club leaders interviewed by the press, political activism reflects the defense of issues dear to historically marginalized groups, such as social minorities, women, and the LGBTQIA+ population.

Political science experts point out that the capillarity and propagation speed of these communities' algorithms were fundamental to breaking through the traditional debate bubble. By adapting the language of the internet and fandoms to the electoral scenario, young people managed to engage voters who usually stayed away from partisan politics. Variety also highlighted that the ballot box result represents a relief for the Brazilian cultural sector, which feared setbacks and drastic funding cuts in the event of a defeat.

As the dust settles after the electoral dispute, the historic performance of Billie Eilish's admirers and other major music names shows that the barriers between entertainment and citizenship are increasingly fluid. The digital mobilization in 2026 has gone on record as a milestone of how pop culture can be channeled to influence the democratic course of a country, consolidating a new era of civic participation led by the younger generations on social media.

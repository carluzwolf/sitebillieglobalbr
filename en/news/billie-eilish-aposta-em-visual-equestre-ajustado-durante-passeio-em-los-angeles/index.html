Billie Eilish opts for fitted equestrian look during outing in Los Angeles

The artist traded her traditional baggy clothes for an all-black riding outfit and is preparing to release a tour film.

By Billie Global BR Newsroom · Dec 1, 2025 · 2 min read

A recent break for the Thanksgiving holiday in Los Angeles yielded moments of leisure away from the stage for Billie Eilish. The singer, known worldwide for her historical preference for baggy and relaxed clothing, surprised onlookers when she was spotted adopting a completely different aesthetic to enjoy the long weekend with her family.

This time, the artist behind the hit What Was I Made For? set aside her logo-filled oversized wardrobe to visit a private equestrian practice area located in the city of Glendale. The purpose of the outing was to practice horseback riding, an activity for which she carefully selected pieces much more fitted to her body than usual.

The attire chosen for the occasion consisted of an all-black look, composed of traditional tight riding breeches and a long-sleeved top with a half-zip collar. To complete the minimalist outfit, the artist wore knee-high boots adorned with metallic buckles.

To finish off the practical production, the young star opted to keep a natural look, without visible makeup, tying her long, dark hair into a ponytail. The choice of fitted clothing marks a subtle transition compared to previous years, when she herself explained that she preferred baggy pieces precisely to safeguard her privacy against public judgment.

While enjoying moments of rest after the holiday festivities, the artist is also making waves behind the scenes in the entertainment and business industry. Recently, she used her social media to make official the arrival of the cinematic recording of her current concert series in theaters in March, in a project co-directed alongside James Cameron.

Beyond the stage and screen, the voice of Hit Me Hard and Soft has drawn attention for her direct stance at public events and speeches directed at figures of extreme wealth. Without hesitating to name names, the singer has publicly questioned the concentration of capital in the hands of billionaires, demanding actions aimed at solving global crises.

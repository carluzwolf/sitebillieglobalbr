Billie Eilish faces criticism on the web after launching luxury jewelry collection

The artist generated controversy on social media by putting high-priced pieces on sale shortly after criticizing the wealth concentration of billionaires.

By Billie Global BR Newsroom · Nov 6, 2026 · 2 min read

Singer Billie Eilish found herself at the center of a controversy on social media over the past few days. Fans and internet users expressed frustration with the artist following the launch of a high-end jewelry line, with prices reaching around $2,400. The negative backlash arose quickly on digital platforms, where many pointed out a contradiction between the products being sold and the pop star's recent public discourse.

The controversy gained momentum because, shortly before the collection was announced, the voice of Hit Me Hard and Soft had made strong statements criticizing the extreme concentration of wealth. In interviews and public appearances, she publicly demanded that billionaires distribute their fortunes and adopt more philanthropic stances to combat social inequality, an agenda she often actively defends tied to sustainability and economic justice.

With the high prices of the pieces made public, part of the internet audience began to question the coherence of the singer's stance. Social media users argued that commercializing such expensive accessories distances the artist from her younger, middle-class fanbase, besides coming into direct conflict with the criticism directed at capital accumulation.

To date, there is no official confirmation of a statement from the singer or her team to respond to the criticism received on the internet. The artist, known for her closeness to fans and for using her platform for environmental and social awareness, has maintained discretion on the matter while the debate continues to divide opinions on social media.

The controversy highlights the rigorous scrutiny that public figures face when addressing complex political and economic issues. While supporters argue that artists need to commercialize products to sustain their teams and operations, critics emphasize that defending egalitarian agendas requires greater alignment between speech and commercial practices.
